A SMALL GUIDE TO BIG FEELINGS

Make yourself at home.

  1. 01

    Bring the parts.

    Play the demo or add your own stems: the separate drum, bass, instrument and vocal files from a song. They all start together.

  2. 02

    Follow your ears.

    Press play. Adjust levels. Use M to mute and S to hear a part on its own. Select a stem to shape its tone, texture and space. Undo is there when you need it.

  3. 03

    Keep what you make.

    Save a project to reopen the individual parts and settings. Export a WAV to keep your finished mix. A project backup is yours to keep even if browser storage is cleared.

Using iPhone or iPad

Tap Play to activate audio. If sound stops after a call or you switch apps, return here and tap Play again. Keep JuiceWeaver in the foreground while editing or exporting.

To install: open in Safari, use the browser’s Share menu, then Add to Home Screen. Your browser manages file downloads through Files.

Files, limits & privacy

Start with separate stems that share the same starting point. A full song can be loaded as one part; JuiceWeaver does not separate its instruments.

Up to 8 stems, 3 minutes each. Maximum 64 MiB per source file, 96 MiB total source audio and 96 MiB of decoded audio per session. Browser codec support varies. WAV is the safest starting point.

Your audio is processed on this device. Local commands use predefined controls, not an AI model. There is no account, tracking or silent audio upload.

Playback, saving & sound

Every stem shares one timeline. Loop repeats the session. Higher effects and combined stems can raise the output level; watch for the peak warning and reduce Output if needed.

Save your project before closing the browser. Session recovery depends on available browser storage and is not a substitute for a downloaded backup.

Audio export uses the same effects as playback. Playback can vary with your browser and output device.

Release candidate: physical iPhone/iPad verification remains outstanding. Browser and emulation results do not replace a device test.
